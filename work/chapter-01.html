01
THE STARTING POINT

A good first investment
starts outside the market.

For your situation, the first decision is how much of your money is truly long-term. Your remaining nine months in the US and your investment horizon are two different clocks.

YOUR CONTEXT

California resident · Vietnamese citizen · STEM OPT · US income-tax resident · About nine more months in the US. The main guide covers US accounts and US-domiciled investments. A US-domiciled fund can hold companies worldwide.

If you need the money in nine months

Prioritize an FDIC-insured high-yield savings account or Treasury bills maturing before you need the cash. Moving costs, tuition, taxes, a deposit and your emergency reserve do not belong in stock funds. A stock ETF can be easy to sell while still being worth much less than you paid.

If you can leave it invested for 10+ years

A low-cost, diversified stock index fund, optionally paired with high-quality bonds, is a reasonable starting framework. You do not need to predict the next winning company. You do need to tolerate a large decline without being forced to sell.

A SENSIBLE DEFAULT TO INVESTIGATE

Fidelity + a cash account + a simple portfolio.

For a US-based beginner, my starting broker choice is Fidelity: low trading costs and recurring dollar-based ETF purchases. Choose the account’s tax treatment first. For long-term stock exposure, investigate VT for a single global fund, or VTI + VXUS for separate US and international holdings. Add bonds according to your risk capacity. This is a starting framework, not a personalized allocation.

Put your next dollar in the right place

  1. 01
    Cover the basics.

    Pay essential bills, keep health coverage, make required debt payments and hold a starter cash buffer. A surprise bill should not force a stock sale.

  2. 02
    Check your employer match.

    Capture an affordable 401(k) match when the benefit is likely to vest. Read the vesting schedule carefully with a nine-month stay; your own contributions are yours, but some employer contributions may be forfeited when you leave.

  3. 03
    Eliminate expensive debt.

    Paying down a 20% credit-card balance avoids a known interest cost. Stock returns are uncertain. The debt-versus-match order depends on liquidity, the interest rate and vesting.

  4. 04
    Build your safety reserve.

    A common baseline is 3–6 months of essential expenses. Consider more when employment and visa status are linked, plus a separate relocation budget. Keep enough in a bank account for immediate access.

  5. 05
    Invest money with time to recover.

    Use eligible retirement accounts for retirement goals; use a taxable brokerage account for flexible long-term goals. Start with an amount you can repeat without drawing down your reserve.

Your nine-month test

Imagine stocks fall 40% the month before you need the money. If that would disrupt your plans, that money belongs in the short-term bucket. A long expected return does not cancel a near-term obligation.

A quick word on your STEM OPT and tax status

This guide takes your US income-tax residency as given; it does not determine it from your visa. F-1 status and tax residency are different systems. Student exclusions from the substantial-presence test usually cover the first five calendar years, with exceptions. Recheck residency for the actual tax year if your circumstances change. [[residency,519]]

For ordinary brokerage reporting, a US tax resident generally provides Form W-9 and receives Form 1099 reporting. Do not choose W-8BEN merely because you are not a citizen. Give the broker accurate citizenship, immigration and tax-residency information. [[w9]]

The activities discussed here are passive investing with your own money. Managing a business, working for a portfolio company or managing other people’s money raises separate employment questions. DHS requires qualifying STEM OPT training and an employer relationship; ownership is not work authorization. Get advice from your DSO or an immigration attorney before taking an active business role. 02
HOW INVESTING WORKS

The container is not
the investment.

A broker, an account, a fund and a stock answer different questions. Keeping them separate makes almost everything else easier.

A

Stocks = ownership

You own part of a business. Returns come from changes in its value and distributions to owners. Profits can grow, but valuations can fall and companies can fail. A stock does not promise repayment.

B

Bonds = lending

You lend to a government or business. A bond specifies payments, subject to the issuer’s ability to pay. Before maturity, its price changes with interest rates, credit risk and other conditions.

C

Funds = a basket

Many investors pool money into a portfolio. A fund can own stocks, bonds, both, or something more complex. Owning several funds does not necessarily mean owning different assets.

The few ideas that do most of the work

Diversification reduces dependence

Thousands of companies reduce the damage from any one failure. They do not prevent a market crash. Geography and asset type matter too: holding ten technology funds can still be one concentrated bet.

Price is different from value

A $20 ETF is not “cheaper” than a $200 ETF in an economic sense. Share count is arbitrary; expenses, holdings and valuations matter. A 2-for-1 split changes the share count, not your wealth.

Total return is the useful measure

Total return includes price changes and distributions. A dividend is not free extra money: a distribution reduces the fund’s assets and, all else equal, its value. A high payout can coexist with a falling account balance.

Liquidity is different from safety

A stock ETF is liquid because you can usually sell it quickly. That says nothing about whether you will recover your purchase price. A Treasury held to maturity has a specified payment date; a bond fund keeps replacing holdings.

Compounding needs time

Reinvested gains can earn further gains. But losses compound too: a 50% decline requires a 100% gain to recover. Smooth illustrations are arithmetic, not how markets actually move.

Inflation changes the goal

If your account grows 5% and prices rise 3%, your purchasing power grows about 1.94%, before tax: 1.05 ÷ 1.03 − 1. A stable dollar balance can still buy less over time.

Roth versus traditional: compare tax rates, not buzzwords

A traditional contribution can be attractive if your marginal tax rate now exceeds the rate when you withdraw. Roth can be attractive in the reverse situation. Future rules, withdrawal timing and location matter. Do not assume a student with a salary is automatically in a low bracket, especially in California.

For 2026, direct Roth IRA contributions phase out at modified adjusted gross income of $153,000–$168,000 for single/head-of-household filers and $242,000–$252,000 for married filing jointly. Married-filing-separately rules are restrictive. For a single filer covered by a workplace plan, traditional IRA deductibility phases out at $81,000–$91,000. These are income tests, not account-balance limits. [[limits]]

Can I withdraw Roth IRA contributions if I need them?

Under federal ordering rules, regular Roth IRA contributions come out before conversions and earnings and generally can be withdrawn free of income tax and penalty. That does not mean the whole balance is freely accessible. Qualified earnings distributions generally require the five-tax-year rule plus age 59½ or another qualifying condition. Conversions have separate rules.

Using a Roth as an emergency account sacrifices scarce retirement space; withdrawing does not usually restore contribution room. Keep contribution records. Early taxable IRA distributions can also trigger a 10% federal additional tax unless an exception applies, and California can impose an additional tax. [[withdraw,capenalty]]

What about a backdoor Roth IRA, rollovers or stock from my employer?

A “backdoor Roth” combines a nondeductible traditional IRA contribution with a Roth conversion. Existing pretax traditional, SEP and SIMPLE IRA balances can make the conversion partly taxable under the pro-rata rule. It is not an extra contribution allowance. Use Form 8606 and get tax help before trying it.

When leaving an employer, compare keeping the old 401(k), a direct rollover to a new eligible plan, or a direct IRA rollover. Cashing it out can create tax and penalties. A pretax-to-Roth conversion is generally taxable.

RSUs, ESPPs and options are compensation arrangements, not a substitute for a diversified portfolio. Your salary and employer stock can fail together. Vesting, withholding and cost-basis adjustments need separate review; broker basis may omit income already taxed on a W-2.

California HSA note

The federal benefits can still be useful, but California generally requires adjustments for contributions and taxes HSA investment earnings. Save account-level income and basis records; your custodian may not produce every state-specific figure you need. Keep near-term medical spending liquid. [[caadjust]]

App-first brokers can work, but do not pick one primarily for signup stock, referral rewards or an eye-catching cash rate. Compare account fees, support, tax documents, transferability and whether the app encourages frequent trading. A robo-adviser can automate allocation and rebalancing, but its advisory fee is in addition to fund expenses. Ask whether the service solves a problem you actually have.

Eight questions to settle before opening

  1. Eligibility: Does the broker accept your actual citizenship, current residence and visa documents? A US SSN does not guarantee approval.
  2. Account: Is this individual taxable, IRA or another account? Does it default to a cash account or margin?
  3. Recurring investing: Can it invest your desired dollar amount in your chosen ETF, not merely transfer cash?
  4. Costs: What are the ticker-specific trade, service, advisory, transfer-out and closure charges?
  5. Idle cash: What holds it, what yield does it earn, and what protection applies?
  6. Portability: Can whole shares transfer in kind? Fractional shares often need liquidation.
  7. Service: Can you reach a person to resolve verification, transfers or tax-document problems?
  8. Security: Are strong multifactor authentication, account alerts, trusted contacts and beneficiaries available?
BANK FAILURE

FDIC

Deposit insurance generally covers $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, per ownership category. Your same-category accounts at the same bank are added together. A bank sweep can qualify subject to its terms and your other deposits at participating banks. [[fdic]]

MISSING BROKERAGE ASSETS

SIPC

At a member broker, protection is generally up to $500,000, including $250,000 for cash, per customer in a separate capacity. It addresses missing assets in broker failure, not investment losses. Money market funds count as securities, not cash, for SIPC purposes. [[sipc]]
